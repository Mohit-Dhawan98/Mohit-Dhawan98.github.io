REET

Research on shrinking an AI agent's context by keeping the facts instead of trimming the words. Paused; small experiments, honestly reported.

Kind
Agentic AI
Where
Open source
Status
research prototype, paused
Links
Repository

The problem

An agent's context keeps growing, and every token is paid for on every call. The usual fixes drop words or sentences, and what they keep can lose its meaning: one baseline turned "1,000 requests per minute" into "1,000 minute".

What was built

A pipeline that cuts a document into chunks, has a language model design an extraction schema for each, and uses GLiNER2, a small local model, to pull out entities, relations and facts as compact text. Around it: one interface for the baseline compressors (truncation, LLMLingua-2, LongLLMLingua, QUITO-X and EXIT) and a question-answering evaluation.

What the experiments found

Everything was measured on small samples, once each, so the results are leads, not evidence.

On ten HotpotQA questions, the best variant kept 27% of the tokens and scored 0.50 F1, against 0.58 for the full context and 0.43 for LLMLingua. Each question is worth about 0.1, so that gap is less than one question. Entities were found; the relations between them mostly were not, and every question that turned on who did what to whom was missed.

Folding the relation into the entity label made the context much smaller, about 7% of the tokens, and less accurate: 0.38 F1, below every baseline.

What it points to

When short entity cards were written outside the pipeline as a ceiling, they carried the answers well, which suggests the format is sound and the extraction is the bottleneck. The next step would be a small model trained to extract complete subject, relation and object triples. It is not built.

Status

A research prototype, paused. The package installs, its tests pass and the truncation baseline runs; the model-based parts were not rerun for the public release. Schema design still calls an API model.
